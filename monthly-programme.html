● Structured coaching, not one-offs

What Is a Monthly Golf Coaching Programme?

The monthly golf coaching programme replaces the stop-start pattern most golfers fall into. Instead of buying a lesson when something goes badly wrong, panicking about it for an hour and then disappearing for four months, you have continuous contact with the same PGA Professional, a plan that runs across a season and a record of your numbers that goes back further than your memory does.

It costs £60 a month. That is deliberately close to the price of a single one to one session, because the point of the programme is regularity rather than volume. Golfers who see a coach every few weeks improve at a completely different rate to golfers who see one twice a year, and the difference has very little to do with how much talent either has.

How Is It Different From Booking Individual Lessons?

A one-off lesson has to solve something inside sixty minutes, which pushes both coach and golfer towards quick fixes. A programme does not carry that pressure. Your coach can spend a session establishing what your body will allow, another rebuilding the strike, and a third moving that change onto the course, knowing you will be back. Bigger, more durable changes become possible because nobody is trying to make them stick in one hour.

The second difference is memory. On a programme your coach knows what you were working on in February, what your carry numbers looked like then, and which drill you never got round to doing. That continuity is worth more than most golfers expect, and it is the main reason people who move from single 1:1 golf lessons onto the programme rarely go back.

What Is Included Each Month?

Regular coaching sessions with your PGA Professional, a written practice plan between each one, Trackman data captured and stored session by session, and video kept so you can compare where you are now to where you started. Your coach also builds your practice around what you can genuinely fit in, whether that is three sessions a week on the Trackman driving range or twenty minutes on a Sunday.

Where a physical restriction is holding back a change, a TPI movement screening and follow-on work in the golf fitness suite get folded into the same plan, so the coaching and the conditioning point in the same direction instead of fighting one another.

What Is OnForm and Why Does It Matter?

OnForm is the video coaching platform your programme runs through, and it is the part that changes how quickly people improve. You film a swing on your phone at the range, it lands with your coach, and it comes back drawn on with a voice note explaining what to do next. The point of it is simple: keep improving between lessons, not just during them.

That matters because the gap between sessions is where most golf coaching quietly falls apart. The feel of a change fades inside a couple of days, and by week three most golfers are rehearsing something subtly different to what they were shown. Having your coach look at a swing on the Tuesday of week two stops a fortnight of practice going into the wrong movement.

How Does the Programme Fit the Evolution Plan?

The Evolution Plan is the four-strand framework the whole academy is built on, and the monthly programme is how most golfers work through it. The first strand is Advanced PGA Professional Coaching, which is there to build lasting improvement through structured coaching with an Advanced PGA Professional, regular reviews and a clear pathway to achieving your golfing goals. That is the spine of the programme.

The second strand is the TPI Golf Movement Programme, which improves your mobility, stability and movement quality with golf-specific exercises designed to help your body support lasting swing changes. Your included TPI screening is what sets that going. The third and fourth strands, Strength & Conditioning and Physiotherapy, sit alongside for golfers who need them, developing strength, power and resilience through golf-specific training, and assessing and treating pain, injuries and movement restrictions that may be limiting your golf. Work in the golf fitness suite is where the physical strands get done.

● How the programme runs

How Does the Monthly Coaching Programme Work?

Five stages, repeating. The first month sets the baseline and everything afterwards is measured against it.

1

Baseline assessment

Your first session captures where you are: full Trackman numbers across the bag, video from two angles and an honest conversation about your goals and your available practice time.

2

Set the target

One or two goals for the season, written down. Break 90, stop the slice, add ten yards, tidy up wedges from 60 to 100 yards. Vague ambitions are impossible to measure and easy to abandon.

3

Coach, then practise

Each session makes one change and hands you a plan to embed it. Between sessions you practise on the Trackman range or the short game area, knowing exactly what you are working on.

4

Measure again

Your next session starts by re-testing the same numbers. If they have moved, the change worked and we build on it. If they have not, we find out why rather than repeating the instruction louder.

5

Take it to the course

Range numbers only matter if they show up on a card. Course management, strategy and on-course sessions around Ormonde Fields get built in once the technical work is holding up under pressure.

● Measuring improvement

How Do You Track Progress in Golf Coaching?

Which Numbers Actually Matter?

Fewer than Trackman offers. For most golfers the useful set is carry distance per club, smash factor, dispersion and start direction. Those four tell you whether you are striking it better and controlling it better, which is what lowers scores. Club path and face angle matter while you are fixing a shape problem and can be ignored the rest of the time. Chasing every metric at once is the fastest way to make a coaching programme feel like homework.

How Quickly Should I Expect to Improve?

Strike quality tends to move first, often within a month, because smash factor responds quickly to a better grip and better low point control. Dispersion follows over two or three months. Handicap moves last and least predictably, because scoring depends on short game, course management and a certain amount of luck as well as ball striking. Judging a programme by handicap alone after six weeks is unfair on everybody involved.

What If I Cannot Practise Much?

Say so at the baseline session and the plan changes accordingly. A golfer with twenty minutes a week gets a different plan to one with five hours, and pretending otherwise just produces guilt and a plan nobody follows. Short, focused practice works surprisingly well: ten minutes of putting on the practice green beats an hour of hitting drivers for most handicaps.

Can I Pause or Change Coach?

Yes to both. Golf goes quiet for some people over winter and busy for others, and the programme should fit your year rather than the other way round. The plan is matched to your goals as they move, so a winter block on technique and a spring block on scoring are the same coach with a different aim. Will Painter, Advanced PGA Professional, teaches the sessions throughout, which is why a change made in November is still being tracked in May.

Golfers travel to the programme from Derby, Nottingham, Ripley, Heanor, Belper and across Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire. Sessions run seven days a week from 7am to 8pm, and every facility on site is covered or floodlit, so a monthly plan does not quietly stop working in November.
