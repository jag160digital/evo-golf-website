What Is a Trackman Driving Range?

A Trackman driving range is a driving range where every bay is fitted with Trackman dual-radar tracking, the same launch monitor technology used on the PGA Tour and by the majority of the world's leading coaches. Instead of hitting a bucket of balls into a field and squinting to see roughly where they finished, you get a precise set of numbers for every shot you hit, displayed on a screen in front of you within about a second of impact.

That difference matters more than most golfers expect. On a conventional range near Derby or Nottingham you are effectively practising blind. You might think you flushed a seven iron, but you have no idea whether it carried 145 yards or 158, whether the spin was 5,200rpm or 8,400, or whether that slight fade was two yards of shape or twelve. Without that information you cannot tell whether the change you just made to your grip actually helped.

What Does Trackman Measure on Every Shot?

Trackman tracks both the club and the ball through impact and reports the full picture. The headline numbers most golfers care about are ball speed, club head speed, smash factor, launch angle, spin rate, carry distance, total distance, shot shape and landing angle. Beyond those, it reports club path, face angle, attack angle and dynamic loft, which is the data your PGA golf coach will use in a lesson to explain precisely why the ball is doing what it is doing.

For a golfer practising alone, the two most useful numbers are usually carry distance and dispersion. Knowing that your eight iron carries 142 yards rather than the 155 you have been telling your playing partners for years is the single fastest way to stop coming up short of greens.

Is a Trackman Range Better Than a Normal Driving Range?

For practice that actually transfers to your scorecard, yes. A standard range gives you repetition. A Trackman range gives you repetition plus feedback, and feedback is what turns repetition into improvement. You find out immediately whether a swing thought is working, rather than believing it worked because one shot out of ten felt good.

It also removes a very common problem: golfers grooving a fault. Without data it is entirely possible to spend a winter hitting two hundred balls a week and get worse, because you are reinforcing the same compensation every time. Numbers catch that early.

Why Practise at a Trackman Range Near Derby?

Most range sessions are wasted because the golfer cannot tell what actually changed. A Trackman range removes that problem entirely. You get a number against every swing, so you know within one shot whether an adjustment helped or hurt.

It also makes practice measurable over months rather than minutes. Golfers who practise here regularly can look back and see clubhead speed climbing or dispersion tightening, which is far more motivating than a vague sense that things might be going alright.

● Getting the most from a session

How Should You Practise on a Trackman Range?

Know Your Carry Distances First

Before working on technique, spend one session simply establishing how far each club actually carries. Hit ten shots with each iron, discard the two worst, and take the average carry. Most golfers discover they are between one and two clubs shorter than they believed, which explains an awful lot of shots that finish in the front bunker.

Practise With a Target, Not Just a Direction

Pick a specific yardage for each shot rather than hitting into open space. Trackman tells you the carry, so you can score yourself honestly. Ten shots to a 140 yard target with a scoring system is worth an hour of aimless ball-beating.

Mix Random Practice Into Your Session

Hitting thirty seven irons in a row is comfortable and mostly useless, because golf never asks you to do that. Change club every shot for part of your session and you will practise the thing the course actually tests, which is producing a good strike first time with a club you have not just warmed up with.

Do Not Chase Every Number at Once

Trackman reports a lot of data and it is easy to get overwhelmed. Pick one metric to work on per session. If you are fighting a slice, watch club path and face angle and ignore everything else. If you want more distance, watch smash factor before you worry about clubhead speed, because striking the middle of the face is worth more than swinging harder.

If you would rather have someone structure that for you, our coaches build practice plans as part of the monthly coaching programme, so you arrive at the range knowing exactly what you are working on.
