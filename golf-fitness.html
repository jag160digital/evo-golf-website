What Is Golf Fitness?

Golf fitness is physical training built around the demands of the golf swing rather than around general health. The swing asks the body to separate the upper and lower halves, rotate quickly, stabilise through impact and repeat that seventy or eighty times in four hours while walking six miles. Training for it looks very little like training for anything else, which is why generic gym programmes rarely move a handicap.

Our approach starts with assessment, not exercise. Before anyone is given a single drill, we look at how your body actually moves, because the exercises that help a golfer who cannot rotate are the opposite of the ones that help a golfer who is too mobile and cannot control it. Golfers from Derby, Nottingham and across Derbyshire come here for that first part more than for the second.

Why Are So Many Swing Faults Physical Rather Than Technical?

Because the body will always find a way to get the club to the ball. If your hips cannot rotate far enough in the backswing, you will slide, or lift, or bend your lead arm, and one of those will show up on video as a fault. A coach can ask you to stop doing it. Your body will simply pick a different compensation, because the original restriction is still there.

The classic examples are familiar to anyone who has had golf lessons and not managed to make them stick. Early extension, where the hips move towards the ball through impact, is very often limited hip internal rotation or weak glutes. Loss of posture is frequently thoracic spine mobility. A reverse spine angle can be a hip flexor problem. Instruction alone does not touch any of those.

What Does TPI Mean?

The Titleist Performance Institute is the industry standard for assessing how a golfer's body relates to their golf swing. Coaching here works to TPI certified principles, which means a structured physical screen read against known swing characteristics. It is the same framework used on tour, and it is where every fitness plan here begins. Full detail is on the TPI screening page.

● The four pillars

What Does Golf Fitness Cover?

Four areas, weighted differently for every golfer depending on what the screening finds.

Mobility

Hips, thoracic spine, shoulders and ankles. Most amateur golfers are restricted in at least two of them, and the swing they end up with is the one their range of motion allows rather than the one they want.

Stability and Strength

Mobility without control is just wobble. Glutes, core and grip strength give you something to rotate against, which is what turns range of motion into a swing that repeats under pressure on the eighteenth.

Speed and Power

Speed is trainable at any age, and it is the single most reliable way to shorten the course. Overspeed work, sequencing drills and measured progress on the Trackman range, so the gains are numbers rather than feelings.

Injury Prevention

Golfers hurt lower backs, lead wrists and elbows, usually through repetition rather than accident. Screening finds the imbalances that cause it before they turn into six weeks off in the middle of the season.

● Distance and durability

Can Golf Fitness Add Distance?

Usually, yes, and often more quickly than technical work does. Clubhead speed comes from how fast you can move and how efficiently you sequence it, both of which respond to training. Golfers who commit to golf fitness for eight to twelve weeks frequently add three to six miles an hour of clubhead speed, and every mile an hour is worth roughly two to three yards of carry with a driver.

The less exciting benefit is durability. A golfer who can still rotate on the sixteenth hits a better tee shot than one who has been quietly falling apart since the turn, and that is worth more over a season than any single swing change. Distance is what people ask for. Playing pain free in ten years is what they thank us for.

● Getting started

How Do I Start Golf Fitness Training?

In order, because doing this the wrong way round is how golfers end up doing exercises they did not need.

1

Book a TPI screening

£75 for a structured physical assessment with a TPI certified coach. It takes about an hour and tests mobility, stability, balance and rotation against the movements the golf swing requires. Book it on the TPI screening page.

2

Match the findings to your golf swing

The screen on its own is only half the picture. Read against video and Trackman data from the teaching bay, it shows which of your faults are physical and which are genuinely technical.

3

Get a plan you will actually do

Usually three or four exercises, ten to fifteen minutes, three times a week. Long programmes get abandoned by week two. Short ones survive, and a short programme done consistently beats a thorough one done twice.

4

Train, then retest

Six to eight weeks of work, then the same screen again alongside speed numbers on the Trackman range. Either the restriction has moved or it has not, and both answers are useful.

5

Fold it into your coaching

Physical work and technical work should be pointed at the same target. Golfers on the monthly coaching programme get both reviewed together rather than treated as two separate hobbies.

● Who it suits

Who Is Golf Fitness For?

Golfers Over Fifty Losing Distance

The most common enquiry we get. Distance loss with age is real, but a great deal of it is lost mobility and lost speed rather than anything inevitable. Both respond to training. Golfers in their sixties regularly regain speed they assumed was gone for good, which changes which clubs they are hitting into greens.

Anyone With a Recurring Back or Wrist Problem

If your lower back aches after every round, that is information rather than bad luck. Screening usually finds a limitation somewhere else, commonly the hips or thoracic spine, that the back has been compensating for. Note that we assess movement for golf and are not a medical service, so anything painful should see a clinician first.

Juniors Who Are Growing Quickly

Growth spurts wreck coordination and timing, and young golfers often need physical work more than swing changes during them. The Junior Academy screens juniors as a matter of course and build age-appropriate movement work in, well before anyone touches a weight.

Ladies and Beginners Building From Scratch

Starting golf with a body that can already rotate and stabilise makes learning far quicker, because you are not learning compensations. The Ladies Academy includes movement work, and new golfers coming through beginner coaching or Get Into Golf can add a screening at any point.
