What Is a Grass Driving Range?

A grass driving range is exactly what it sounds like and rarer than it should be: an outdoor practice area where you hit off natural turf rather than a synthetic mat. Ours sits on the ground at Ormonde Fields Golf Club in Codnor, Ripley, and it is open through the drier months of the year, with the covered Trackman range bays carrying the load in winter.

Most golfers in Derby, Nottingham and across Derbyshire do the overwhelming majority of their practice on mats, then wonder why the ball behaves differently on the course. The reason is not mysterious. A mat and a fairway respond to a slightly heavy strike in completely opposite ways, and only one of them tells you the truth.

Why Do Mats Hide a Fat Shot?

Because the club bounces. Hit two inches behind the ball on a mat and the sole skids off the surface into the back of the ball, and the shot comes out somewhere close to normal. Do the same on turf and the club digs, the divot starts before the ball, and you have a chunk that finishes forty yards short. The swing was identical. The feedback was not.

Over a winter of mat practice that adds up. Golfers groove a low point behind the ball without ever being told, then arrive in April convinced they have lost their game overnight. They have not. They have been practising a fault that the surface was covering up for them.

What Is Low Point Control and Why Does It Matter?

Low point is where the clubhead reaches the bottom of its arc. For an iron off the fairway it needs to be in front of the ball, so you strike ball first and take the divot after. It is the single most important factor in decent iron play, and it is the thing grass measures for you automatically. The divot tells you where the low point was, in the ground, permanently.

That is why coaching moves outdoors when the weather allows. A golf lesson on strike is far more productive on turf, and the Trackman data from a grass shot reflects a real contact rather than a mat-assisted one.

What Does Real Turf Tell You That a Mat Cannot?

Three things, all of them immediate. Where the club entered the ground, how deep it went and which way the divot points. None of that survives contact with a synthetic mat, because the mat is designed to be forgiving and the fairway is not. Golfers who move from mats to grass usually find their strike is a shade heavier than they believed, and finding that out on a practice ground beats finding it out on the first tee.

It also changes club selection. Long irons, hybrids and fairway woods off the deck are a completely different proposition on turf, and they are the clubs most golfers hit worst precisely because they never practise them from the ground. An hour out here with a five wood is worth a season of hitting drivers into a net.

When the ground needs recovery time, the work moves indoors to the covered Trackman driving range bays, and coaching carries on in the Trackman teaching bay where strike can be measured rather than guessed at.

● Grass against mats

Is Grass Better Than Mats for Practice?

For strike, yes, without much argument. A grass driving range gives honest feedback on low point and contact quality, and it does it on every single shot without anybody having to tell you. Mats give you volume, comfort and year-round availability, and they are kinder to your wrists and elbows over a long session.

The sensible answer is that you want both, used for different jobs. Do your repetition work and your data work in the covered bays, then test the strike on turf before it matters. Golfers who only ever practise on mats tend to be worse at ball-first contact than they think, and golfers who only ever practise on grass tend to stop practising in November.

How Do I Use the Grass Driving Range?

Nothing complicated, but a call before you set off in changeable weather will save you a wasted journey.

1

Check it is open

The grass range is seasonal and weather dependent. It opens when the ground can take traffic and closes when it cannot, which in Derbyshire is a moving target. Ring 07710 582036 on the day if the weather has been unkind.

2

Park and pick up a basket

Free parking on site at Ormonde Fields Golf Club, Nottingham Road, Codnor. Baskets run from £4.00 for 30 balls to £12.50 for 120. The larger baskets are better value per ball if you intend to spend real time on it.

3

Work along the turf, not into one hole

Take your divots in a line, working backwards or sideways so you use fresh ground each shot. It keeps the surface usable for the next golfer and gives you a clean lie every time, which is the point of being out there.

4

Watch the divots, not just the ball

After each shot, look at where the divot started relative to where the ball was. That is your low point, and it is more useful information than the flight, particularly with mid and short irons.

5

Move indoors when the weather turns

When the ground is too wet or the light has gone, the covered Trackman bays are floodlit until 8pm every day, and the golf simulator is booked by the hour.

● Seasons and weather

When Is the Grass Range Open?

It Is Seasonal, and We Are Honest About That

Natural turf needs recovery time and it will not take heavy traffic when it is saturated. The grass range therefore runs through the drier part of the year and closes when conditions demand it. Anyone claiming a grass range in the East Midlands is open every day of the winter is describing a field they are about to ruin.

What Happens When It Is Closed?

Practice carries on indoors. The Trackman driving range has seven covered, floodlit bays open 7am to 8pm every day of the year, with full ball and club data on every shot. That is the year-round facility, and for most golfers it is where the bulk of the work happens.

How Do I Know Before I Travel?

Call 07710 582036. It takes thirty seconds and it beats driving over from Derby or Nottingham to find the ropes across. If you are coming for coaching, your coach will decide on the day whether the session is better served on turf or in the teaching bay.

Is It Worth Building a Trip Around?

If your iron striking is the weak point, yes. An hour on grass in June will tell you more about your contact than a winter of mats. Pair it with the short game area and you have half of a genuinely useful practice morning, with the covered simulator and indoor bays covering the rest.
