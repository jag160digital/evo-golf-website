● Titleist Performance Institute screening

What Is TPI Screening?

Sometimes it's the body, not the swing. Sixteen gentle TPI tests show us how your movement shapes your game.

TPI screening is a structured physical assessment developed by the Titleist Performance Institute, used by the majority of tour players' support teams. It takes your body through a sequence of movement tests and grades each one, producing a clear picture of what you can and cannot currently do. The point is not fitness for its own sake. The point is that your swing is limited by your body long before it is limited by your understanding.

That connection is the whole idea behind TPI. There is no single correct golf swing, but there is a correct swing for your body, and a golfer who cannot separate their upper body from their lower body will not produce a tour-style transition no matter how many lessons they buy. Screening finds the restriction first, so coaching stops trying to fix something that is physically unavailable.

What Does the Screen Actually Test?

Sixteen tests, covering hip mobility and internal rotation, thoracic spine rotation, shoulder mobility, hamstring and hip flexor length, ankle mobility, core stability, balance and the ability to dissociate the upper body from the lower. Each test is graded and the results are written down rather than remembered, so they can be re-tested in three months and compared.

It takes around an hour and involves no golf swings for much of it. You will be asked to squat, rotate, reach and balance, all in ordinary clothes. Nothing is strenuous and nothing requires any existing fitness. Golfers regularly discover that the shoulder turn they have been chasing for two years is unavailable because their thoracic spine will not rotate, which is a far more useful thing to know than another swing tip.

Which Swing Faults Come From the Body?

A great many of the common ones. Early extension, where the hips push towards the ball through impact, is frequently a hip mobility or glute activation problem rather than a technique fault. Loss of posture in the backswing often traces to limited thoracic rotation. Casting and early release regularly come from poor core stability or wrist mobility. Sway and slide usually indicate weak lower body stability. In each case, coaching the symptom without addressing the cause produces a change that lasts about a fortnight.

That is why screening pairs so directly with coaching. Golfers on the monthly programme often start with a screen, because it tells the coach what the plan can realistically ask for, and it stops six weeks being spent on a movement the golfer's body was never going to make.

● The turning point

Why Does the Academy Screen the Body First?

The answer wasn't the swing, it was the body. That sentence is the reason Evolution Golf Academy exists at all. Will Painter's own playing career stalled, not because he did not understand what he was trying to do with the club, but because his body simply wasn't capable of consistently producing the movements he was trying to make. Years of coaching had been aimed at a position his hips and thoracic spine were never going to let him reach.

Fixing mobility, stability and movement quality alongside the coaching is what changed that, and it is what the whole academy is now built on. It is also why a TPI screening is not sold here as an add-on for tour hopefuls. It is the assessment that tells a coach whether the next six months of lessons are going to work.

Step 2 - The TPI Movement Screen

Assessment at the academy runs in two steps. Step 1 is the PGA Professional golf swing assessment, using Trackman, video analysis, 3D data and coaching experience. Step 2 is the TPI movement screen, and it identifies:

  • mobility or stability restrictions affecting your swing
  • movement patterns limiting your performance
  • physical limitations preventing swing changes
  • areas that could increase power, consistency and efficiency
  • whether further support from a fitness professional or physiotherapist would help

Put the two steps side by side and the argument usually settles itself. If Step 1 says your club is coming in from the inside and Step 2 says your lead hip will not rotate internally, you have your answer, and it is not another hour of 1:1 golf lessons spent trying to feel a position. Screening is carried out by Will Painter, PGA Professional · TPI Certified · Trackman Expert, and he is the only coach at the academy.

What Is Custom Club Fitting on Trackman?

The other half of the equipment question. Once we know what your body does, it is worth knowing whether your clubs suit the swing that body produces. ● How a screening works

What Happens During a TPI Screening?

About an hour, five stages, and no golf clubs needed for most of it.

1

Talk through your history

Injuries, surgeries, back trouble, desk job, other sports and how much you currently move. Context changes how results are read, and a stiff thoracic spine in a lorry driver is not a mystery.

2

Work through the movement tests

Squats, rotations, reaches and balance tests, each one graded. Ordinary clothes are fine and nothing is strenuous. You will not be asked to do anything you cannot do.

3

Connect the results to your swing

Each restriction is matched to the swing characteristics it commonly produces, then checked against what your swing actually does on video and on Trackman.

4

Get your report and plan

A written record of every graded test plus corrective exercises targeting your specific limitations. Most plans need fifteen minutes a few times a week rather than gym membership.

5

Re-test and adjust

Mobility work is measurable. Coming back in eight to twelve weeks and re-running the same tests shows what has changed, and the swing plan is updated to use the movement you have gained.

● Who benefits

Who Should Have a TPI Screening?

Golfers Who Keep Making the Same Fault

If a fault returns within two weeks of every lesson, technique is probably not the problem. Early extension and loss of posture in particular are usually the body finding a way round a restriction. Screening tells you which restriction, and at that point the fault stops being a mystery and starts being a solvable one. This is the largest single group who benefit.

Golfers With Back, Hip or Shoulder Trouble

The golf swing asks a lot of the lower back, and a body that cannot rotate through the hips and thoracic spine will take that rotation from the lumbar spine instead, which it is not designed to provide. A screen identifies that pattern before it becomes an injury. Screening is an assessment rather than medical treatment, and anything clinical gets referred on rather than guessed at.

Golfers Chasing Distance

Clubhead speed is produced by sequencing and by the range through which you can accelerate. If your hips will not rotate internally, or your lead shoulder will not reach across, you are capped regardless of effort. A screen shows where the ceiling is and the golf fitness suite is where you go and raise it. Speed work on top of a restriction just produces a faster bad movement.

Golfers Over Fifty, and Juniors

Both ends of the range gain a lot. Older golfers get a swing built around what their body currently allows rather than one copied from a twenty-five year old tour player. Juniors get movement quality established early, which prevents years of compensation. Will Painter runs the junior academy and says when a screen would be worthwhile for a young player.
