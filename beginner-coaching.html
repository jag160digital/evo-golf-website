● Golf lessons for complete beginners

What Do Beginner Golf Lessons Include?

Beginner golf lessons start from the assumption that you know nothing about golf, because most people booking them do not. You will not be asked what your handicap is, you will not be expected to own clubs, and nobody will use words like dynamic loft at you in the first hour. The single aim of session one is that you leave having hit some golf balls decently and wanting to come back.

The order rarely changes, because it works. Grip first, since almost every fault a new golfer develops is a grip fault wearing a disguise. Then posture and stance, then aim and alignment, then a small swing with a wedge. Only when the ball is coming off the middle of the face at half speed do we lengthen the swing, and only then does the driver come out of the bag. Golfers who start with a driver spend two years undoing what they learn in that first fortnight.

Do I Need Any Experience or Equipment to Start?

None at all. Clubs are provided for beginner sessions, so you can turn up in trainers with nothing in your hands and start learning. That also means you avoid the classic beginner mistake of spending £600 on a set before anyone has watched you swing. Once you know whether golf is for you, your coach can advise on what to buy, and brand neutral custom fitting with Trackman is there when you are ready, so you can buy from us or anywhere you like.

What Should I Bring to My First Golf Lesson?

For your first lesson, we recommend bringing your golf clubs, comfortable athletic wear, and a positive attitude. If you don't have clubs, we can provide them for you.

Wear something you can turn in and shoes with a bit of grip. Golf shoes are useful eventually but not on day one. There is no dress code at the academy that is going to catch anybody out, which is a reasonable thing to worry about and worth saying plainly. Club hire on the Trackman driving range and the Trackman simulators is included at no extra cost, so practising between lessons costs you nothing more than a basket of balls.

Where Do Beginner Lessons Take Place?

Usually in the covered Trackman teaching bay, which is private, indoors and out of the wind. That matters for new golfers more than for anybody else, because learning in front of a row of low handicappers on an open range is nobody's idea of a relaxing hour. Later on, sessions move out to the Trackman driving range and the short game area once you would rather see the ball fly.

Beginner coaching is available one to one at £55 an hour, or as part of the monthly coaching programme at £60 a month if you want a steady run of sessions rather than a single lesson. If the idea of a private lesson feels like too much attention, Get Into Golf runs as a relaxed group where everyone present is equally new.

● The first four things

What Will I Learn in My First Golf Lessons?

The fundamentals, in the order that makes each of the next ones easier. There is nothing clever here, which is exactly why it works.

Grip

How you hold the club decides where the face points at impact, and where the face points decides where the ball goes. Get this wrong and you spend years building compensations around it.

Stance and Posture

Distance from the ball, width of stance, spine angle and balance. Dull to read about, and the difference between striking the ground behind the ball and striking the ball first.

Aim and Alignment

Most new golfers aim well right and then swing left to compensate. Learning to aim at the target removes a fault before it has a chance to become a habit.

A Repeatable Swing

A short, balanced swing you can repeat beats a long one you cannot. We build length gradually once the strike is consistent, usually with a wedge or a seven iron.

● From the bay to the first tee

How Do I Get From Lessons Onto a Golf Course?

This is the step most beginners get stuck on, and it is rarely about technique. People stop because they do not know how a tee time works, what to wear, how fast to play or what happens if they hit it sideways in front of strangers. Every one of those worries is normal and every one of them is answerable in about five minutes.

We take beginners out onto the course at Ormonde Fields once you can make contact reliably, often playing three or four holes rather than eighteen. You learn course management, pace of play, where to stand, how to keep score and the handful of etiquette rules that actually matter, all in the place they apply rather than in a bay.

What Happens in Your First Beginner Lesson?

Sixty minutes, start to finish, so you know exactly what you are walking into.

1

Arrive and meet your coach

Free parking on site at Ormonde Fields Golf Club in Codnor. Will Painter, PGA Professional · TPI Certified · Trackman Expert, meets you at the bay, and the first five minutes are a chat about why you want to play, not a test of anything.

2

Get clubs in your hands

If you have no equipment, clubs are provided and your coach picks something suitable. A seven iron or a wedge to begin with, because both are far easier to strike than a driver.

3

Grip, stance, aim

The three fundamentals, taught in that order and rehearsed a few times without a ball. It feels strange for about ten minutes and then stops feeling strange for good.

4

Hit your first shots

Small swings, quiet expectations. Trackman is running, but the numbers stay off the screen unless you want them. Most beginners are hitting the ball in the air inside twenty minutes.

5

Leave with a plan and no pressure

You get two drills and a suggestion for what to do next, whether that is another lesson, the monthly programme or simply a basket of balls on the range to see if you enjoyed it.

How Long Does It Take to Learn Golf?

How Long Before I Can Hit the Ball Consistently?

Most beginners are getting a wedge airborne inside their first session and hitting a seven iron with some regularity within three or four. Consistency across a full set takes longer, generally three to six months of occasional practice. Anyone quoting you a fortnight is selling something. The pace depends far more on how often you practise than on natural talent, which is genuinely good news for most of us.

How Long Until I Can Play a Full Round?

Six to twelve lessons and a bit of practice will usually get you round nine holes without feeling out of place. A full eighteen is more about stamina and pace of play than technique. Playing a few holes with a coach first, as part of on-course work, shortens that timeline considerably because the worry rather than the swing is what holds people back.

What Golf Etiquette Do I Actually Need to Know?

Less than you fear. Keep up with the group in front rather than worrying about the group behind. Stand still and stay quiet while somebody is swinging, and stay out of their eyeline. Repair your pitch mark on the green and rake the bunker after you. Shout if a ball is heading anywhere near another person. That short list covers ninety per cent of it, and nobody at a golf club minds a beginner who is trying.

Am I Too Old to Start Playing Golf?

No, and this comes up constantly. Golf is one of the few sports where people take it up in their fifties and sixties and still improve for years. If mobility is a concern, a TPI screening shows what your body can currently do, and the swing is then built to suit you rather than to suit a tour player. Work in the golf fitness suite can widen those limits over time.

Beginners come to the academy from Derby, Nottingham, Ripley, Heanor, Alfreton and across Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire, and a fair number arrive having been given a set of clubs they have never used. That is a perfectly reasonable way to begin. If you would rather see the whole picture first, the FAQs page answers most of the practical questions people ask before booking.
