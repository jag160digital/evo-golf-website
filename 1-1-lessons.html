● One to one PGA coaching

What Happens in a 1:1 Golf Lesson?

Just you and a PGA pro for an hour. We'll look at your Trackman numbers, film your swing, and send you off with a clear, simple plan.

A 1:1 golf lesson here runs for a full sixty minutes in a private Trackman bay, and it opens with a conversation rather than a swing change. Your coach will ask what your golf currently looks like, what your handicap has been doing, which shot you are frightened of standing over, and what you actually want from the game. A golfer chasing a first round under 90 needs a very different hour to one trying to stop losing three balls a side off the tee.

Then you hit shots, and nothing is altered while you warm up, because the first job is to see what you do when nobody is interfering. Trackman records ball speed, launch angle, spin rate, carry distance, club path, face angle and attack angle on every strike, and the camera captures what your body is doing to produce those numbers. Inside fifteen minutes your golf coach has a measured picture of your ball striking rather than an impression of it.

How Does a Golf Coach Decide What to Work On?

By working backwards from the ball flight. If the ball starts left of target and curves further left, the face was closed relative to the path through impact, and the numbers say so precisely rather than approximately. Your coach then hunts for the cause: grip, aim, ball position, a body restriction, or in a surprising number of cases equipment that does not fit the golfer holding it. That last one comes up often enough that we run custom club fitting on Trackman on the same site.

One change per lesson is the working rule. A golfer handed four swing thoughts leaves the bay feeling well informed and plays worse for a month. A single clear, measurable change gives you something you can rehearse on the Trackman driving range the following week and prove to yourself actually worked.

Are Golf Lessons Recorded and Sent to Me?

Yes. The video from your session, the relevant Trackman numbers and the drills you were given all come to you afterwards. That matters more than it sounds, because the feel of a change fades inside about 48 hours while the footage does not. Being able to compare a down the line clip from March against one from January is how most golfers realise quite how far they have moved.

Sessions can cover full swing, driving, iron play, wedge distance control, bunker play or putting. Short game work moves out to the short game area, and on-course coaching around Ormonde Fields can be arranged when course management is costing you more shots than technique, which for mid handicappers it very often is.

● Booking a lesson

How Do I Book a Golf Lesson Near Derby?

Booking takes about a minute, and you do not need to know a single thing about your own swing beforehand.

1

Choose a coach and a time

Book online or ring 07710 582036. If you are unsure what to book, describe what you are struggling with and we will match the session to that rather than selling you the longest block.

2

Arrive ten minutes early

Parking is free on site at Ormonde Fields Golf Club, Nottingham Road, Codnor. Ten minutes gives you time to find the bay and loosen up instead of swinging cold at your first shot.

3

Talk before you swing

The opening few minutes cover your goals, your typical miss and your golfing history. Be honest about how much you can realistically practise, because the plan is built around your week, not an ideal one.

4

Hit, measure, change, measure again

Your coach sets a baseline on Trackman, makes one change and shows you the numbers moving. Feeling different and being different are not the same thing, and the data settles that argument in seconds.

5

Leave with a written plan

Video, key numbers and drills follow by message. Most golfers book their next session two to four weeks out and use the grass driving range or the covered bays in between.

● Getting value from coaching

How Often Should You Have Golf Lessons?

Is One Golf Lesson Enough to Fix a Slice?

Occasionally, yes, because a startling share of slices trace back to a grip that is too weak or a set-up aimed thirty yards right of target, and both can be changed inside an hour. More commonly one lesson identifies the cause and begins the change, and two or three more embed it. Anybody promising a permanent cure without watching you hit a ball is guessing.

How Many Lessons Do Most Golfers Take?

Golfers attacking one specific fault usually book three or four sessions across a couple of months. Golfers who want steady long term improvement tend to move onto the monthly golf coaching programme instead, because regular short contact beats an intensive burst followed by six months of nothing. Two to four weeks between lessons is the normal gap and it gives you room to practise before the change goes stale.

What Should I Bring to My Golf Lesson?

Your own clubs if you own some, because your coach wants to see what you genuinely play with, including the driver you do not trust. Golf shoes or trainers with grip, and clothes you can turn in. We can lend you a set if you have none at all, so a first lesson is entirely possible with nothing but yourself and a pair of trainers. There is no dress code waiting to catch you out.

Do I Have to Practise Between Sessions?

It helps enormously, though far less of it is needed than most people fear. Two focused twenty minute sessions will beat one aimless two hour bucket every time. Your plan names the drill, the number of balls and what a good repetition looks like, and the Trackman driving range is walk-in from 7am to 8pm so it can be squeezed around work.

If your body is what stops you making the movement rather than your understanding of it, that becomes obvious quickly. In that case a TPI screening and some structured work in the golf fitness suite will do more for your swing mechanics than another hour of instruction ever could.
