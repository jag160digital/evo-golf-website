Short game practice near Derby & Nottingham

Around half of the shots in a round happen inside 100 yards, yet most golfers spend almost all of their practice time hitting driver. That is the single easiest thing to fix.

Our short game area gives you real turf lies around a proper green, a practice bunker with real sand and a true rolling putting green, so you can practise every scoring shot you actually face.

Add a short game lesson with our Advanced PGA Professional and you will know exactly which shots to practise and how.

Why Does Short Game Practice Drop Handicaps Fastest?

Because the shots are shorter, the margin for improvement is enormous and the physical demands are low. A mid handicapper who gets up and down four times a round instead of once has saved three shots without changing their swing, gaining a yard of distance or spending a winter rebuilding anything.

It is also the part of the game where practice transfers most directly. A forty yard pitch on our practice green is the same shot you will face on the course, off a similar lie, to a similar target. There is very little translation loss, which is not true of hitting fifty drivers off a flat mat.

What Can I Practise Here?

Chipping and pitching from real turf lies around the green, bunker play from a practice bunker with real sand, and putting on a true rolling surface where pace and read behave the way they will on the course. Between them, that covers every shot you hit inside 100 yards apart from the full wedge, and the Trackman range next door covers that one.

Bunker practice deserves a particular mention, because it is the shot most club golfers avoid practising and consequently the one that costs them most. A practice bunker with real sand means you can hit fifty splash shots in an afternoon and turn your worst shot into an ordinary one. A short game lesson is usually the quickest way to make that happen.

How Should I Structure a Short Game Session?

Aimlessly dropping a dozen balls in one spot and chipping them at the same flag is the most common mistake, because it rehearses a shot you will never face twice in a round. Play each ball from a different lie and to a different target instead, exactly as the course will present them, and score yourself out of ten on getting up and down.

Finish with putting, and spend most of that time on pace from long range rather than on three footers. Three putts cost far more shots across a season than missed short putts do, and pace is the skill that transfers between greens. Half an hour structured that way is worth more than two hours of unfocused chipping, and it is the routine our monthly programme members are given to work through between lessons.

● Top questions from golfers

Short game practice Derby & Nottingham FAQs

Do I need to book the short game area?+
No. It is walk in, seven days a week alongside the rest of the academy, from 7am to 8pm. There is no booking, no membership and no minimum spend.
Are balls provided?+
Yes, balls are provided on the green. Bring your own wedges and putter, since practising with your own scoring clubs is the entire point of the exercise.
Is there a bunker?+
Yes, a practice bunker with real sand so you can work on greenside shots properly. It is the facility most golfers say they need and least often have access to.
Can I have a short game lesson?+
Yes. Book with our Advanced PGA Professional and we will build the session around your scoring shots, then give you a practice plan that targets the ones costing you most.
Is it suitable for beginners?+
Very. Short game is often the quickest confidence win for a new golfer, because a decent chip is achievable in a single session in a way that a decent drive usually is not.
Where is it?+
Ormonde Fields Golf Club, Nottingham Road, Codnor, Ripley DE5 9RJ, between Derby and Nottingham and minutes from Ripley, Heanor and Ilkeston.
