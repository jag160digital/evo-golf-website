What Is a Trackman Golf Simulator?

Play Pebble Beach whatever the weather. Indoor simulator bays, hired by the hour, all year round.

Play famous courses, practise in the dry or join a league night. There are two premium, private Trackman simulators at the academy, both booked by the hour, both open from 7am to 8pm seven days a week, and both fifteen minutes from Derby city centre.

A golf simulator is an indoor bay where a launch monitor measures what the club and ball actually do, then puts the resulting shot onto a screen in front of you. Ours run on Trackman, the same dual-radar system used on the PGA Tour, which means the ball you hit into the screen behaves on the software the way it would have behaved outdoors on a still day in Derbyshire.

For golfers in Derby and Nottingham that solves a very British problem. There are roughly four months of the year when eighteen holes is a test of character rather than a test of golf. An indoor bay gives you somewhere warm, dry and floodlit to keep hitting shots, and unlike a mat in a garage it tells you exactly what those shots did.

How Does a Golf Simulator Actually Work?

You hit a real golf ball with your own clubs into a screen a few yards away. Trackman's radar tracks the club through impact and the ball through the first part of its flight, then models the rest from the measured launch conditions: ball speed, launch angle, spin rate, spin axis and attack angle. Because it measures rather than guesses, a thin three iron looks like a thin three iron on screen, and a well struck one runs out the way it should.

Is It the Same Technology as the Driving Range Bays?

It is the same launch monitor and the same measurements you see in the Trackman driving range bays, doing a different job. The range is about volume and immediate feedback. The simulator wraps that data in a golf course, a target and a score, which changes how you behave. Golfers concentrate differently when there is water short of the green, even an imaginary one.

If you want the numbers explained rather than simply displayed, that is a coaching conversation, and it happens next door in the Trackman teaching bay with an Advanced PGA Professional.

● Accuracy and honesty

How Accurate Is a Golf Simulator?

Accuracy depends almost entirely on what is doing the measuring. Camera-only systems infer a great deal from very little. Trackman's dual radar measures club delivery and ball launch directly, then applies a flight model that has been checked against real shots for years. In practice, carry distances on our golf simulator in Derby and Nottingham sit within a couple of yards of what the same swing produces outdoors.

The honest caveat is that a simulator cannot give you wind, a downhill lie, or the particular horror of a four footer to win the match. It measures the shot accurately. It does not replace the golf course. As practice and as winter mileage it is excellent, and most golfers get more out of an hour indoors than out of a summer of unstructured range sessions.

● Booking a bay

How Do I Book a Golf Simulator Session?

Unlike the outdoor range, the simulator is booked in advance. Bays get busy from October onwards, so a few days of notice through the winter is sensible.

1

Choose your slot

The academy is open 7am to 8pm, seven days a week, and the simulator runs the whole time. Early mornings and mid-afternoons are quietest. Evenings from six are the busiest, particularly between November and February.

2

Book online or by phone

Book through the online system or call 07710 582036. If there are more than four of you, ring rather than book online so we can put you in the right bay and give you enough time.

3

Decide what you want from the hour

Playing a course and practising a skill are two different sessions. Deciding beforehand stops the hour quietly disappearing into nine holes that nobody scored.

4

Arrive ten minutes early

Parking is free on site at Ormonde Fields Golf Club, Nottingham Road, Codnor. Ten minutes gives you time to change shoes, warm up and get the bay set up without eating into your booking.

5

Add coaching if the data raises questions

Golfers regularly book a simulator hour, see something in the numbers they cannot explain, and follow it with a one to one golf lesson. That is the sequence we would recommend, in that order.

● Who uses the bays

Who Is the Golf Simulator For?

Club Golfers Who Refuse to Stop in Winter

By some distance the largest group. Golfers carrying a handicap who know from experience that three months off costs them the first half of the following season. An hour a week indoors keeps sequencing, strike and clubhead speed roughly where they were in September, and the difference shows in April.

Beginners Who Would Rather Not Be Watched

A simulator bay is enclosed and quiet, which suits anyone still nervous about topping one in front of strangers. It works well alongside beginner golf coaching, and if you have genuinely never held a club, Get Into Golf is a gentler first step than booking a bay on your own.

Groups, Societies and Anyone Organising Something

Nine holes on a famous course, four players, a scorecard and no possibility of rain makes a straightforward evening out. Groups from Derby, Nottingham, Ripley and across Derbyshire use the bays for society nights, birthdays and the annual attempt to settle an argument about who is actually the longest hitter.

Juniors and Families

Short attention spans and long waits do not mix. On the simulator a junior gets a shot every thirty seconds and a score to chase, which holds interest far longer than a basket of balls hit into a field. The Junior Academy uses the bays for exactly that reason, and the Ladies Academy runs indoor sessions once the light goes in October.

Which Simulator Price Tier Applies to Me?

Two tiers, and the difference is worth planning around. Off-peak is Monday to Friday from 8:00am to 2:00pm and starts at £13 an hour for one player, rising to £24 for four. Peak is Monday to Friday from 2:00pm to 10:00pm plus the whole weekend, and it is a flat £25 an hour for the bay whatever the group size. Four players at peak works out at a little over six pounds each, which is still the cheapest hour in golf.

If all you want is to hit balls rather than play a course, range baskets on the Trackman driving range run from £4.00 for 30 up to £12.50 for 120, and a private Trackman range bay with ball data, club data and video analysis is £15 an hour.

How Long Should I Book For?

An hour covers nine holes for two players, or a focused solo practice session. Four players wanting eighteen holes should budget two hours and probably a little more, because golf on a screen still involves people telling stories between shots. If in doubt, book the longer slot.

Is Coaching Included in the Price?

No, coaching is booked separately. A one to one lesson with an Advanced PGA Professional is £55 an hour, and the monthly coaching programme is £60 a month if you would rather have continuous support than occasional lessons. Prices can change, so confirm on 07710 582036 before you travel.
